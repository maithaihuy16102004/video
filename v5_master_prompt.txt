# PART I — PERMANENT VISUAL STYLE LOCK

## 1.1 APPROVED STYLE

The permanent approved art direction is:

**PREMIUM STICKMAN CINEMATIC — STYLE B**

This style must be used throughout the V5 production pipeline.

It combines:

- Expressive, professionally designed 2D stickman characters.
- Organic ink-like linework.
- Simple but distinctive character silhouettes.
- Stylized faces with expressive eyes, eyebrows and mouths.
- Character-specific hairstyles, clothing and accessories.
- High-quality articulated animation.
- Rich stylized 3D environments.
- Cinematic lighting and camera direction.
- Strong visual storytelling.
- Consistent color grading and typography.

The characters must remain recognizable as stickmen.

Do not convert the art direction into realistic human anatomy, generic anime, Pixar-style 3D characters or low-effort geometric figures.

## 1.2 CHARACTER DESIGN RULES

Character anatomy:

- Simple stylized head.
- Minimal torso with intentional silhouette.
- Elegant tapered limbs.
- Clean elbow and knee articulation.
- Simplified expressive hands.
- Distinctive hairstyles and accessories.
- Clothing that clearly identifies character identity and context.

The character must be recognizable from silhouette alone.

At least five distinct character archetypes must be available at launch.

Character identity must not depend exclusively on clothing color.

Support variations in:

- Head proportions.
- Body proportions.
- Posture.
- Hair.
- Costume.
- Facial expression.
- Movement personality.
- Accessories.
- Cultural and historical context.

## 1.3 LINEWORK RULES

Use a consistent illustrated line system:

- Smooth Bézier curves.
- Controlled variable stroke widths.
- Clean rounded line joins where appropriate.
- Anti-aliased vector rendering.
- Stable outlines during animation.
- Consistent visual weight across camera distances.
- No jitter from unstable geometry.
- No raw debugging shapes in production output.

Line thickness must be configurable according to resolution, camera framing and visual style.

Do not force every body part to use identical stroke width.

## 1.4 ENVIRONMENT DESIGN RULES

The world should be more detailed than the stickman characters.

Three.js environments must provide:

- Readable architectural silhouettes.
- Spatial depth.
- Appropriate props.
- Ground contact.
- Motivated light sources.
- Atmospheric perspective.
- Material variation.
- Weather effects where relevant.
- Environmental motion.
- Foreground, midground and background composition.

The scene must communicate its location even when subtitles and title cards are hidden.

Do not substitute unrelated generic architecture when the scene requires a specific environment.

## 1.5 CINEMATIC PRESENTATION

Apply:

- Strong composition.
- Readable staging.
- Motivated camera movement.
- Consistent lighting direction.
- Character/background contrast.
- Purposeful depth of field.
- Controlled color grading.
- Shot-to-shot continuity.
- Natural pacing.

Never sacrifice story readability for visual effects.

## 1.6 STYLE ENFORCEMENT

Create a machine-readable VisualStyleBible configuration.

All characters, environments, lighting presets, materials, graphics and production templates must reference this configuration.

The frontend must expose the active style as:

`Premium Stickman Cinematic — V5`

Do not allow the engine to silently change art styles between scenes.

Future style changes require explicit configuration versioning.
